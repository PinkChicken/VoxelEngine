### DESIGN (2026-07-04): connected-vessel hydrostatics — SOLVED IN A 2D BENCH via pressure projection; four cheaper approaches falsified first

**Status: validated design, not yet ported to GLSL.** After Build B (below) was reverted, the U-tube/connected-vessel problem was worked out from scratch in a standalone 2D Python emulator (a faithful bench: same owned-face flux model, same conservative `s_out`/`s_in` transport as `fluid_flux.comp`, plus a strict per-arm-mass + full-column + mass-conservation test suite). The bench falsified **four** successive approaches with concrete mechanisms before landing on one that passes every invariant. This entry records all of it so the next attempt doesn't re-walk the dead ends — **especially so nobody rebuilds a max-relaxation field like Build B again; the bench proves that whole family cannot work, and explains exactly why.**

**The core requirement the bench isolated:** the solver needs *a spatial pressure gradient generated by the current mass imbalance* — not a propagated maximum height. Any method that produces a pressure/head field which is uniform-at-each-elevation across a connected body has, by construction, deleted the lateral gradient that must drive water from the heavy arm to the light one.

**The falsification ladder (all ruled out, do not retry):**

1. **Narrow "static lift into air" correction (Y-only, gated on local equilibrium).** Fails two ways, both proven in-bench: (a) a *full horizontal pipe* has zero head gradient (every cell reads `head=255`, air above) so dynamic flux physically cannot route the tall arm's pressure across it; (b) the instant a lift starts, the donor goes partial and local `head()` sees an "unsupported" column and pulls it back down. Can't route, can't climb.
2. **Deficit toward a surface-elevation field (`L = y + fill/255`, fill cells below `L`).** Conserves mass but **smears**: a single scalar surface elevation can't tell "supported by a connected column" from "should pool lower," so water spreads as thin partial films up to the stale surface instead of pooling full. Also `L` (a no-decay max) never lowers when the source arm drains.
3. **`P` replaces `head()`, `P` = max-with-cost relaxation (`+FILL_CAP` per upward hop, propagate through liquid), rebuilt per tick from live seeds.** This is the **Build B family done more carefully** — and it is *equilibrium-correct but dynamically dead*. `max`-propagation computes a reachable upper envelope, so once it fills a connected region with the same maximum potential it makes `P` **uniform at each elevation** → the bottom-pipe horizontal gradient is exactly zero → the arms freeze lopsided (measured: bottom-row `P = [2040,2040,2040,2040]` at a 2:1 mass imbalance). **This is why Build B never worked, now isolated on a bench rather than guessed at.** No tuning rescues it; the defect is the `max` operator, which erases gradients. Rule: **never use a max/envelope-propagated field to *drive* equalization flow.**

**What finally worked — quasi-static pressure projection (Chorin) on the fill field.** Pressure is a *constraint correction to a provisional flux*, recomputed every tick, never stored as momentum; transport stays the only thing that moves fill. Per-tick pipeline:
- **Provisional gravity flux**, scaled by the water's actual **weight** (`fill`), on faces with liquid above (a flat constant gives a topology-only field that can't distinguish arms).
- **Divergence of that provisional flux** as the base RHS — this is what differs between a tall and a short arm and generates the lateral gradient.
- **Density-correction source** `STIFF·(FILL_CAP − fill)` on **submerged** cells (liquid directly above), **masked off at the free surface** (air above). Without this term: a thin column sits in false hydrostatic equilibrium and never fills (the "constant-at-elevation" trap). *Unmasked*, it force-fills legitimately-partial surface cells and breaks the rest state. **Both the term and the mask are load-bearing** — the bench fails specific invariants if either is dropped.
- **Iterative pressure solve** (`∇²P = RHS`) with BCs: `P = 0` at liquid→air (free surface, Dirichlet); solid **and** withheld/unavailable chunk pages are **sealed** (Neumann, face dropped — *never* treated as air/`P=0`, same rule as the dispatch-range fix below); air excluded from the domain.
- **Correct** `F = u* − ∇P`, feed the existing conservative `s_out`/`s_in` transport unchanged.

**Bench invariants it passes (the regression suite for any future port):** pressure-null at rest (corrected flux exactly 0 on a settled column); **bottom-pipe gradient exists** under unequal arms (the direct anti-test for the max-field failure); flat-lake no-op; **U-tube equalizes to level-matched FULL columns**, per-arm mass balanced, mass-exact; final level **independent of pipe length** (no horizontal decay); unsupported thin film drains instead of pinning; settled partial-surface column stays dead quiet (no churn). Conservation drift was `0.000` in every test, including the broken ones — the `s_out`/`s_in` transport is mass-exact by construction regardless of the driving field, which is what let the bench cleanly attribute every failure to the *field*, not the transport.

**⚠️ Metric pitfall — judge equalization by PER-ARM MASS, never `column_top` (this fooled a reviewer, and fooled the author three times):** a lopsided *thin smear* reaches the same column HEIGHT in both arms at very different DENSITIES, so `column_top` (or "median submerged fill over both arms") reports "equalized" while the arms hold very different amounts of water. The correct falsifier is per-arm mass (`sum(fill)/FILL_CAP` per arm column) plus a fill-profile dump. Approach #3 (max-relaxation) *looks* equalized by `column_top` on both the pre-filled-pipe and empty-pipe geometries (`column_top` ≈ equal in both arms) but has arm-mass imbalance ≈ 2–3 with a checkerboarded thin profile — that is the falsification, and it holds on the harder empty-pipe case too (water must migrate across an initially-empty connector; it still ends lopsided). Note: `tools/fluid_prototypes/utube_pressure.py` originally shipped with weak `column_top`-based tests that printed a **false** `ALL TESTS PASSED`; it has been rewritten to self-demonstrate the falsification via the strict metric (`demo_max_relax_falsified()` → `FALSIFIED AS DOCUMENTED`). Do not trust a green "pass" without a per-arm-mass check and a profile dump — build both into the in-engine acceptance scene.

**GPU-port plan (tiered; the cost lives entirely in the solver, so optimize the solver's DOMAIN, RESOLUTION, ITERATION SCHEDULE, and INITIALIZATION — never weaken the pressure model, the RHS, or the surface mask, which the bench proved are doing real physical work):**
- *Tier 1 (do first, fits the existing sparse+substep architecture):* **(a) active-region mask** — only solve pressure-active liquid (nonzero divergence, near a free surface, near a topology/fill edit, or in the halo of an active cell); this is a direct extension of the existing `_build_flux_chunk_list`/idle-guard sparsity, and the "inactive/withheld neighbour = sealed, never air" rule is *literally* the dispatch-range fix already in the code. **(b) temporal amortization** — run a few local solve iterations every tick (kills compression artifacts/thin-film-pinning immediately) and let long-range connected-vessel pressure crawl over *many* ticks via warm-started partial solves + residual-driven adaptive iteration counts (`priority ≈ max(|div|, |fill−FILL_CAP|, surface proximity)`, a "needs-more-solve" bit per page). A U-tube taking ~0.5s to equalize is invisible to a player; a one-frame explosion is not. This is also the only lever that reduces the **per-tick dispatch/sync count** (each solve iteration is a dispatch+sync; active-masking shrinks work-per-dispatch but not the number of syncs).
- *Tier 2 (later, gated on evidence):* coarse `2³` pressure grid for **bulk** bodies only (a `4³` block *erases* a 1-voxel pipe — dangerous for the exact plumbing case this whole effort targets; only safe if gameplay channels are reliably ≥2–3 voxels wide — an open design question); lightweight multigrid V-cycle for long pipes (principled, but real implementation risk on a sparse/irregular/free-surface domain — defer until temporal amortization is shown insufficient).
- *Tier 3 (optional, maybe never):* graph "pipe mode" — collapse degree-2 liquid chains into graph edges and solve a small graph Laplacian over junctions. Strong for long 1-wide plumbing, lots of edge cases; temporal amortization likely makes it unnecessary.

**Shader-specific caveat the roadmap must respect:** the bench used **serial SOR (Gauss-Seidel, `ω≈1.7`)**, which *does not exist on a GPU* (sequential dependency). The port must use **red-black Gauss-Seidel** (checkerboard coloring: update red reading black, then black reading red — parallel, keeps most of SOR's convergence rate) or plain **Jacobi** (ping-pong buffers like `voxels_a`/`voxels_b`, ~2× more iterations). **The iteration counts measured in the bench will not transfer** — re-measure convergence in a red-black/Jacobi bench before sizing the GPU iteration budget.

**Where the validated 2D emulator lives:** built in the session scratchpad (`utube_projection.py` plus the falsification-ladder files `utube_emulator.py`, `utube_pressure.py`). Scratchpad is ephemeral — the design above is complete enough to rebuild it, but the emulator is worth committing into the repo (e.g. `tools/fluid_prototypes/`) as a permanent regression rig before the GLSL port, since it's what will tell us whether the 3D/red-black version still passes the invariants.

### REVERTED (2026-07-04): "Build B" connected-vessel hydrostatics (persistent piezometric-head relaxation field) — handed off for a different approach

**Status: reverted in full, verified clean.** `fluid_flux.comp` and `fluid_simulation.gd` are back to the pre-Build-B state (matching commit `ac86f04`, "Compress fluid flux pipeline from 4 GPU passes to 2"), except that one independently-discovered bug fix (below) was kept. In-editor re-testing after the revert (sphere drops, pouring, settling) confirmed the baseline is stable again: no growth, no leaks, no cellular-automaton-style artifacts. This entry documents what was tried, what broke, and why it was abandoned rather than iterated on further — so the next attempt (handed to Fable) doesn't retread the same ground. **The underlying U-tube/connected-vessel hydrostatics goal is still unsolved.**

**Original goal:** the fluid pipeline settles flat lakes and falling water correctly but fails the classic U-tube test — pour water into one arm of a U-shaped pipe and it never climbs the other arm to find its level, because `head(p, mat)` is a strictly-local, straight-up-only column scan with zero visibility into pressure queued behind a horizontal connection (full derivation was in this entry before the revert; see git history of this file if needed).

**What was built:** `phead(p)` — a new, persistent, relaxation-maintained field standing in for `head()` in the accel formula's `dh` term only. Maintained by a new first pass, Relax-phead (`params.pass == 0`, shifting Accelerate to 1 and Transport to 2): `phead_new(p) = max(head(p), max(0, neighbour_max - PHEAD_DECAY))`, where `neighbour_max` is the elevation-adjusted max over same-liquid 6-connected neighbours' last-tick `phead`, `PHEAD_DECAY = 4` a flat (not multiplicative) per-hop decay. Validated first in a standalone Python prototype (vertical-column/flat-lake no-regression, U-tube propagation, drain-recovery, Jacobi/Gauss-Seidel parity all passed) before being ported to GLSL. New buffer `_phead_buf` rode the same page allocation as `_flux_buf`.

**What went wrong in real in-editor testing (not caught by the Python prototype or small-scale single/two-voxel GPU tests, which all matched predictions exactly):**

1. **Severe mass loss at scale.** Dropping a sphere into a multi-chunk-deep "test tube" dug through rock caused total fill (`debug_dump_fluid_stats`) to collapse across five `F6` snapshots: 972188 → 261744 → 27711 → 5155 → 1919 (>99% of the water). Root cause found and fixed (kept after the revert, see the entry below): `compute_s_out`/`compute_s_in` (from the 4→2 compression, not Build B itself) can read a cell 2 hops from the dispatching thread, but the per-tick dispatch range only guarantees 1-hop visibility — a cell outside that radius was treated as "unlimited capacity" instead of "unknown," a silent leak that `phead`'s much-wider, persistent pressure propagation triggered constantly (where `head()`'s narrow local-only reach rarely had before).
2. **The leak fix alone did not resolve the underlying behavior.** After applying it, further testing (sphere dropped into a dug test-tube shaft) still showed: water visibly "boiling" — voxels launched upward above the surface, falling, sticking, bubbling up again, repeating in a cycle; continued substantial mass loss even with the dispatch-range leak closed; and, per the most direct test (pour into one side of a narrow connected shaft), water spraying out the top of one arm rather than equalizing with the other — i.e. the core U-tube goal (level-matching between connected arms) still did not work correctly, and the system was actively unstable rather than merely imprecise.
3. **Assessment:** the max-relaxation `phead` design is likely fundamentally mismatched with the existing Accelerate/Transport tuning (`g_dt`, `gravity_bias`, `axis_cap`) — those were calibrated against `head()`'s narrow, single-column-bounded range, and `phead`'s legitimately much larger magnitude for real connected bodies (potentially many thousands of fill-units for a multi-chunk-deep body, vs. `head()`'s low hundreds) seems to overdrive them into oscillation rather than settling. This was a working hypothesis at the time of the revert, not confirmed — worth investigating directly if picked back up.

**Decision:** rather than continue iterating on `phead`/Relax-phead in place, the whole approach was reverted back to the known-working 4→2-compression baseline, and the connected-vessel hydrostatics problem is being handed to Fable for a fresh design pass. The glass material and its raymarch rendering (added the same session, for visually inspecting exactly this kind of test — see the Current To-Do entry) were kept; they're independent of the fluid physics and still useful for whatever hydrostatics approach comes next.

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-08, kept after the Build B revert above): `compute_s_out`/`compute_s_in` treated a dispatch-range boundary as "unlimited capacity" instead of "unknown" — real mass leak, not Build-B-specific

**Status: fixed and verified.** Reapplied on top of the reverted (pre-Build-B) `fluid_flux.comp`. This bug lives in the 4→2 pass compression itself (`ac86f04`), not in Build B — Build B just happened to trigger it far more often (see above), which is how it was found. In-editor testing with the reverted-to-baseline fluid pipeline (sphere drops, pouring, settling) confirmed the fix holds cleanly on its own, with no growth/leak/CA-artifact regressions.

**Root cause:** `compute_s_out(X, ...)`/`compute_s_in(X, ...)` each read `X`'s own page via `resolve_page_local`, and — per their own header comments — can be called on a cell up to **2 hops** from the Transport thread that invoked them (Transport calls `compute_s_in(receiver)`, which internally calls `compute_s_out(donor-of-receiver)`). But the per-tick dispatch range (`_build_flux_chunk_list`, `fluid_simulation.gd`) only ever includes **active chunks + their 1-hop face-neighbours** — chunks further out get their `flux_page_table` entry deliberately unmapped (`-1`) for the tick, even if they hold real, persistent flux/fill state from previous ticks (this per-tick unmap/restore is intentional and correct elsewhere — see the dispatch-range-leak fix referenced in `_build_flux_chunk_list`'s own comment). Both functions had `if (page < 0) return 1.0;` for this case — "assume full capacity" — which let a Transport thread credit or debit fill against a neighbour-of-a-neighbour that was invisible this tick and therefore *never dispatched to apply the matching half of the transfer*. Fill vanished (or, at a receiving boundary, appeared from nowhere) exactly at the edge of the 1-hop dispatch radius.

**Fix:** flip both early-outs from `return 1.0;` to `return 0.0;` — "cell outside this tick's visibility ⇒ assume no capacity ⇒ don't transport across this boundary this tick," instead of assuming unlimited capacity. This stalls flow at the dispatch-range edge for at most one tick and self-heals once the frontier chunk becomes active/mapped next tick (same self-healing philosophy as the pre-existing page-allocation-up-front fix documented in `_build_flux_chunk_list`). No dispatch-range widening needed, and no change to `compute_s_out`/`compute_s_in`'s existing legitimate `total_in <= 0.0 → return 1.0` case (that one means "no inflow at all," not "cell invisible this tick," and is unaffected).

**Rule to remember:** any function that can read state belonging to a cell *further away than the current pass's guaranteed dispatch radius* must treat "can't see it" as "assume the worst / no capacity," never "assume the best / unlimited" — the former fails safe (a one-tick stall), the latter fails as a silent, unbounded mass leak that only shows up once some driving force pushes real flux that far (in this case `phead`, but any sufficiently strong forcing term could trigger the same gap).

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-03): exported .exe renders black — shader `.comp` files not bundled into the PCK

**Symptom:** In-editor everything renders correctly. After exporting a standalone Windows `.exe` (Project → Export, "Water Simulation" preset), the running game is **completely black except for the player sphere and the UI** — the entire voxel world is missing. No crash, no visible error window.

**Root cause:** Every compute shader in this project is a plain-text `.comp` file loaded at runtime via `FileAccess.open("res://shaders/*.comp", FileAccess.READ)` and compiled from source through `ComputeHelper.compile_shader*` (see `RaymarchRenderer._shader_source()`, `WorldOrchestrator`, `edit_dispatcher`, `granular_simulation`, `fluid_simulation`). These files have **no `.import` sidecar** — Godot does not treat them as resources. The export preset used `export_filter="all_resources"` with an empty `include_filter`, and `all_resources` only bundles *imported resources* (`.tscn`, `.gd`, textures, …). Non-resource files like raw `.comp` are included **only** if they match `include_filter`. With it empty, `res://shaders/*.comp` was absent from the exported PCK. At runtime `FileAccess.open` returned `null`, so `RaymarchRenderer._shader_source()` returned `""`, SPIR-V compilation failed, and the raymarch pipeline never ran → black screen. The player sphere (a real mesh node) and UI (CanvasLayer) don't go through the raymarcher, so they still drew. This affected *all* shaders, not just the raymarcher — it's just the most visible one.

**Fix:** Set the export preset's non-resource include filter (`export_presets.cfg`, and equivalently the Export dialog → Resources tab → "Filters to export non-resource files/folders to include"):
```
include_filter="shaders/*.comp, shaders/*.glsl"
```
Re-export. Verify by running the exported build with the console wrapper (`debug/export_console_wrapper=2`, already on) — no `RaymarchRenderer: failed to load shader` push_error and a rendered world means the files are bundled.

**Rule:** Any file loaded via `FileAccess.open("res://…")` rather than `load()`/`preload()` is a *non-resource* file as far as export is concerned and will silently be missing from the exported PCK unless it matches the preset's `include_filter`. When adding a new raw asset (shader, JSON, CSV, etc.) loaded by path, extend `include_filter`. Alternative long-term fix (not taken): rename shaders to `.glsl` and load them as `RDShaderFile` resources so `all_resources` picks them up automatically and Godot's `#include` preprocessing becomes available.

**Loose end worth tightening (not required for the fix):** most shader loaders dereference the `FileAccess` handle with no null check (`edit_dispatcher`, `granular_simulation`, `fluid_simulation`), so in the broken export they would have thrown "Cannot call method 'get_as_text' on a null value" instead of failing gracefully like `RaymarchRenderer` does. Adding a null guard + `push_error` to each would make a future missing-file misconfiguration self-diagnosing.

### CHANGED (2026-07-06): fluid pipeline compressed from 4 GPU passes to 2 (Accelerate → Transport)

**Status: implemented and verified** (settled-lake zero-flux check, pour/spread/settle, and — after the dispatch-range leak fix a few entries above — stable at scale with no growth/leak/CA artifacts, confirmed 2026-07-04). Not a bug fix — a structural simplification of the passes described in the Scale-Inflow entry below. `Scale-Outflow` and `Scale-Inflow` used to each be their own fully-synced dispatch, rewriting `flux_buf` lanes (once scaled for the donor's outflow budget, once more for the receiver's inflow budget), which required a careful sign-based proof that no two invocations ever wrote the same lane in the same pass (see that proof in git history / the Scale-Inflow entry below).

**The change:** neither scale factor is written to a buffer by its own pass anymore. `fluid_flux.comp` now has `compute_s_out(gpos)`/`compute_s_in(gpos)` — pure, read-only functions of that tick's already-synced `flux_buf` + `voxels_a`, callable for *any* cell, not just "my own". Transport calls these inline for itself and for whichever neighbor is the true donor/receiver of each of its 6 relevant faces (`compute_s_out` only ever needs a 1-hop neighborhood; `compute_s_in` calls `compute_s_out` on up to 6 neighbors, so from Transport's thread it's a bounded 2-hop lookup, never deeper — no recursion risk). Because both endpoints of a shared face compute the identical deterministic function of the identical stable inputs, conservation holds exactly with zero shared writes and zero races — no proof needed, purity does the job instead of careful bookkeeping.

**Why this is safe re: the sub-threshold trap bug (see MIN_FLOW/eff_flux history further down):** `eff_flux()`'s read-side deadzone is unchanged and still applied identically everywhere `flux_buf` is read (Accelerate's decay, `compute_s_out`, `compute_s_in`, Transport's final delta) — the fix for that bug never depended on which pass did the reading, only on both sides reading through the same threshold function, which is still true here.

**Semantic change worth watching:** since nothing but Accelerate ever writes `flux_buf` now (no Scale pass clamps-and-rewrites it mid-tick), a persistently unservable lane no longer gets nudged toward zero by that rewrite. It's still bounded every tick by Accelerate's own `axis_cap` clamp, so it can't grow unboundedly, but it may sit at a stable nonzero raw value indefinitely if the underlying head difference driving it never resolves (e.g. a permanently pressurized face into an already-full receiver) — this is the physically correct behavior, not a regression, and it structurally deletes the "Scale-writeback oscillating trap" bug class described below (nothing rewrites `flux_buf` mid-tick anymore, so that race can't recur).

### FIXED (2026-07-04, root cause found 2026-07-03): fluid never fully settles — no hydrostatic equilibrium in Pass A's vertical forcing

**Status: fix applied 2026-07-04, verified in-editor same day — CLOSED.** `shaders/fluid_flux.comp`'s Pass A Y-axis accel now uses `(dh - FILL_CAP) * g_dt * gravity_bias` instead of `dh * g_dt * gravity_bias - gravity_force`. `gravity_force` is deleted entirely — the `@export` in `fluid_simulation.gd`, the `SimParams` field in the shader, and the corresponding byte in `_flux_params_buf`/`_upload_flux_params()` (struct/buffer shrunk 56→52 bytes, `settle_threshold`'s offset shifted 52→48 accordingly). No other pass changed — `axis_cap`, the truncated-decay logic, and the neighbour-page dispatch check were all confirmed unrelated to this bug.

**Verification (2026-07-04, in-editor, all 3 scenarios confirmed):**
1. Flat lake at rest: a watched voxel below the surface read `fill=255, flux=(0,0,0)` on load, with zero movement — no oscillation, exact fixed point, matching the emulator's prediction.
2. Pouring water onto the settled surface: spreads outward, forms a low-fill film on top of the stable sheet, and fully settles back to quiet within roughly a minute — no runaway, no standing oscillation.
3. Disturbing a settled surface near a watched voxel (sphere destruction/removal nearby): the watched voxel stayed rock-stable at `fill=255, flux=(0,0,0)` throughout the entire disturbance, completely undisturbed by the chaos a few voxels away. The large per-tick `[Conservation]` numbers during the disturbance are legitimate redistribution of displaced water re-settling (they taper off and stop as the region restabilizes), not a resurgence of the old leak.

Also added this session: a red wireframe indicator box (`world.gd`, tracks `FluidSimulation.watch_chunk`/`watch_local`, visible through geometry via `depth_draw_never`) so the watched voxel is visible in-game while testing, reusing the existing `_cursor_indicator` pattern from `player_controller.gd`.

**The actual mechanism:** Pass A's Y-axis accel is `dh * g_dt * gravity_bias - gravity_force`. `head()` is pressure head only (fill + column above). For two vertically stacked at-rest full cells (B below, A above), `head(B) - head(A)` is **always exactly 255** — not zero. So a column at perfect hydrostatic rest generates a constant, nonzero forcing on every interior vertical face, every tick, forever (with this session's defaults: `255*0.15*3.0 - 10 = +104.75`, upward). `gravity_force` is a flat constant bolted onto the force term instead of something that scales with and cancels against the pressure term the way real hydrostatic balance requires — so "rest" was never actually a fixed point of the system.

Mechanically, this produces exactly what `FluidWatch` showed: interior faces request the same residual every tick, Scale-Inflow clamps it back to 0 (receiver has zero capacity), Pass A re-integrates the same residual from 0 next tick — looks quiet but is permanently spring-loaded. At the *surface* (a full cell below a partial cell), a few units actually transfer up each tick, which drops the full cell below 255 — and `head()`'s walk treats a non-255 cell as "the surface," so `head()` collapses discontinuously (255+f → ~254), flipping the force's sign. Scale then clamps the reverse flow to exactly the deficit, restoring the full cell to exactly 255 — repeating the cycle forever. This is why `damp` was irrelevant to the whole session's tuning attempts: the oscillation isn't underdamped momentum, it's regenerated from scratch every half-cycle by a genuine equilibrium error, and Scale overwrites the stored flux with clamped values every tick regardless of `damp`. It also explains why lowering `damp`/`gravity_force` made it *worse*, not better — that changed the constant residual's magnitude/sign, not its existence.

**The "tearing" event (Symptom 2, see below) is very likely the same root cause discharging at scale:** the entire bulk of a settled body carries a standing, Scale-Inflow-suppressed upward request on every submerged face simultaneously. An edit opens up capacity somewhere in the connected region (air, a non-full cell), and every previously-suppressed request across the whole region stops being zeroed at once — the pent-up flux executes in a single tick. The `~10-20`-per-voxel losses seen during tearing are mostly legitimate redistribution; the small genuine loss component is `round(raw*s)` overshoot in the two Scale passes (clamped away by Transport), individually tiny but summing to thousands of units when thousands of voxels discharge in the same tick. (Worth double-checking separately: whether a widespread event like this can exceed `MAX_CHANGELOG` — dropped entries would apply a donor's debit without the receiver's credit, reading as spatially random tearing while the GPU conservation counter still shows ~0, a different bug wearing the same symptom.)

**The fix (applied 2026-07-04):** gravity for a vertical face now enters as *elevation head* (piezometric head, `h = z + p/ρg`), not a bolted-on constant force. In fill units, one cell of elevation = `FILL_CAP` (255):
```glsl
float accel = (axis == 1)
    ? (float(dh) - float(FILL_CAP)) * params.g_dt * params.gravity_bias
    : float(dh) * params.g_dt;
// delete the separate `accel -= params.gravity_force;` branch entirely
```
This also made `gravity_force` (the export, and its `SimParams` field) unnecessary — its intended purpose (give a floating, near-zero-`dh` mass a baseline pull) is served by the `-FILL_CAP` term itself: a floating blob's bottom face sees `dh_total = 0 - P - 255`, strongly negative, so it falls coherently with no constant force and no equilibrium violation. `gravity_bias` stays (now purely a response-to-imbalance amplifier; the Y-axis `axis_cap` asymmetry is harmless once the underlying equilibrium error is gone — confirmed by testing, not just reasoned). Removed alongside the fix: the `SimParams`/params-buffer size reduction (mirror image of the earlier 52→56 byte bump when `gravity_force` was added) and its `@export` in `fluid_simulation.gd`.

**Verification method (high confidence, not just code-reading):** an independent, exact integer-arithmetic Python emulation of all 4 passes (truncate-toward-zero decay, round-and-store Scale-Outflow/Scale-Inflow with their sign-based ownership rules, Transport's clamp) reproduced this session's actual watched trace bit-for-bit (surface fill cycling `241→237→238→237→236...`, incoming +Y lane alternating `+3 ↔ -3/-4`) under the current shader logic at every parameter set tried (session defaults, the lowered damp/gravity_force experiment, and `gravity_force=0`) — never settles, always a limit cycle. The same emulator with the elevation-head form above reaches an *exact* fixed point at t=0 for a settled column (bit-identical forever) at any `g_dt`/`gravity_bias`, and a mid-air droplet falls at full speed, lands, and fully settles (all lanes exactly 0) within 5 ticks, mass conserved throughout.

**Separate, pre-existing, lower-priority behavior to watch after the fix lands:** a 1-cell-thick surface film still spreads laterally into same-level air (`accel = fill*g_dt`, `MIN_FLOW=1` lets any nonzero through) — a lateral deadzone/threshold question, unrelated to this bug, not worth chasing preemptively.

### SUPERSEDED BY ROOT-CAUSE ENTRY ABOVE — original unresolved writeup, kept for the session's diagnostic trail

**Status when written: actively unsolved.** This is the current top-priority fluid issue. Both leaks fixed earlier this session (dispatch-range in Pass A, missing inflow cap in Scale) are confirmed resolved — this is a *different*, still-open problem uncovered once testing moved to a large, uniform, flat body of water (`ChunkManager.water_depth`) specifically to isolate settling behavior from terrain irregularity.

**Symptom 1 — standing oscillation, confirmed via `FluidWatch`:** a settled-looking watched voxel's own 3 owned flux lanes (`fx, fy, fz`) read exactly `0` on every tick — completely quiet — yet its `fill` value keeps oscillating forever in a clean, non-damping pattern (e.g. `239 → 236 → 240 → 236 → 239...`), driven entirely by the *incoming* neighbour-owned lane from the voxel directly below it (added to `FluidWatch`'s output this session specifically to catch this — see the `in_X`/`in_Y`/`in_Z` fields), which itself alternates roughly `+3/+4 ↔ -3/-4` every tick, forever, never decaying to zero. This is a genuine undamped, period-2 ("checkerboard") standing oscillation at the seam between the top two fill layers of a settled body — not a threshold trap (values are well above `MIN_FLOW`) and not measurement noise.

**Symptom 2 — separate, larger "tearing" event:** under conditions not yet fully isolated (observed after disturbing an otherwise-settled surface with an edit — add/remove a voxel — while the standing oscillation above was already present), a much larger, spatially widespread loss occurs: multiple *different* fully-saturated (`fill=255`) voxels across several chunks each lose a modest amount (~10-20) in the same tick, aggregating to thousands of units lost in a single tick, visually resembling tearing/glitching across a broad area with brief "corners bumped up a voxel" artifacts. `GainTracker` only ever reports the single worst offender per tick, so the true scope (how many voxels are involved) is likely underrepresented by the printed numbers.

**Ruled out this session:**
- Not the `settle_threshold` sleep feature — confirmed to persist identically with `settle_threshold=1` (old "any nonzero change keeps chunk awake" behavior).
- Not simply an underdamped oscillator — lowering `damp` to `0.85` and `gravity_force` to `10` (from `0.97`/`35`) did **not** fix it; the watched trace looked *more* chaotic afterward (larger swings, `fill` jumping 255→148 in one tick, spikes appearing on X/Z lanes too, not just Y). This rules out "just needs more damping" as a complete explanation — the mechanism is structural, not a tuning problem.
- The chunk that stays perpetually active after a fresh rebuild (`R`) with an undisturbed, freshly-generated flat lake is very likely a direct consequence of Symptom 1: the standing oscillation alone is enough real, nonzero activity to prevent the idle-guard from ever seeing 4 consecutive quiet ticks, independent of `settle_threshold`.

**Also found and fixed in the process (not the main bug, but a real bug in the diagnostic tooling itself):** `debug_conservation_check`'s reset of `_conservation_buf`/`_gain_tracker_buf` used to live inside the same `if debug_conservation_check:` gate as the read+print. Transport's `atomicAdd` into that buffer runs every tick regardless of the toggle, so leaving it off let the buffer silently accumulate unread/unreset for as long as it stayed off — the first read after re-enabling it dumped the entire backlog as if it were one tick's loss (a reported "45814 lost this tick" that was actually an accumulated backlog, not a real single-tick event). Fixed by always resetting the buffer every tick, independent of whether that tick's value is actually read/printed.

**Leading (unconfirmed) hypothesis for Symptom 1, not yet investigated in code:** the Y-axis asymmetric clamp (`axis_cap = FILL_CAP * gravity_bias` for Y vs. plain `FILL_CAP` for X/Z, added during the earlier gravity_bias/gravity_force work) combined with the two-stage Scale-Outflow → Scale-Inflow split may be able to amplify rather than damp a Y-axis perturbation under certain fill/head configurations — this is a guess based on the oscillation being Y-axis-specific in the observed traces, not something confirmed by reading the shader carefully yet. Needs a proper code-level investigation, not more empirical tuning.

**Diagnostic tooling built this session specifically to chase this (kept, working, useful going forward):**
- `FluidSimulation.watch_enabled` / `watch_chunk` / `watch_local` (exported, Inspector-editable) — per-tick dump of one voxel's fill + own 3 flux lanes + 3 incoming neighbour-owned lanes (`_debug_dump_voxel()` in `fluid_simulation.gd`). The incoming-lanes part was added specifically because a voxel's own lanes reading zero doesn't mean it's not being driven by a neighbour.
- **F9** keybind (`world.gd`) — sets `watch_chunk`/`watch_local` to whatever voxel is under the crosshair (reuses the same cursor raycast as add/remove voxel), since manually typing coordinates was too unreliable for targeting a specific misbehaving voxel found by eye.
- `[GainVoxel]` auto-dump — every tick the conservation checker reports a nonzero delta, it now automatically calls `_debug_dump_voxel()` on the *exact* flagged voxel, live, same tick. Manually retargeting `watch_chunk`/`watch_local` after reading a previous tick's log line always lagged behind the actual worst offender, since the flagged location moves every tick.
- `FluidSimulation.debug_conservation_check` (default `false`) — gates the conservation-checker's GPU readback (a real per-substep sync-stalling cost), independent of the always-on reset fix above.

**Rule:** when a per-tick GPU-side accumulator (atomicAdd) is paired with a GDScript-side toggle that gates *reading* it, the *reset* must never be gated behind the same toggle — otherwise toggling the read back on will report a misleading accumulated backlog instead of that tick's real value.

### TOOLING (2026-07-03): voxel sleeping via settle_threshold, and debug print toggle

**Problem 1 — perf: a large, mostly-settled lake never fully slept.** `FluidSimulation`'s idle-guard evicts (and zeroes) a chunk's flux page after 4 ticks with a fully-quiet dirty bitmask, letting it drop out of `_active_chunks` and stop being dispatched. But `flux_buf` intentionally persists across ticks (so waves/slosh don't instantly damp), which means a large settled body can keep making tiny (1-3 unit) but genuinely nonzero transfers forever — every one of them reset the idle counter under the old "any nonzero change = still active" rule, so a big lake effectively never slept even once it looked completely flat.

**Fix:** added `settle_threshold` (new `SimParams` field, `FluidSimulation.settle_threshold` export, default 3) — a second, coarser floor purely for the *scheduling* decision, separate from `MIN_FLOW`/`eff_flux()` in the shader (which still own conservation correctness, untouched). Transport still writes every real transfer to `voxels_a` regardless of size — no new leak risk — but only calls `mark_dirty()` when `abs(applied) >= settle_threshold`. Sub-threshold sloshing no longer resets a chunk's idle counter, so it accumulates idle ticks and gets evicted through the existing path — which already zeroes the flux page on eviction. Sleep and zero-residual-momentum happen together by construction, so a chunk that wakes later (e.g. new water added nearby) starts from a clean zero instead of replaying stale, possibly many-tick-old flux values — deliberately chosen over freezing-in-place after user input, specifically to avoid a woken voxel appearing to "remember" expired momentum.

`settle_threshold = 1` reproduces the old behavior (any nonzero change keeps a chunk awake indefinitely) if it ever needs to be dialed back for comparison.

**Problem 2 — perf: unconditional per-substep GPU readback.** The automatic conservation checker (`_conservation_buf` download) ran every single sub-step regardless of whether anything was printed, forcing a GPU sync each time — worth it during the active leak investigation, a real cost once both leaks (dispatch-range, Scale-Inflow) were fixed and it was just running in the background.

**Fix:** gated the whole conservation-check block (download, print, gain-tracker download, buffer resets) behind `FluidSimulation.debug_conservation_check` (new export, default `false`). Re-enable from the remote Inspector only when actively chasing a new conservation issue.

### BUG (2026-07-02): fluid flux leak — Scale only capped outflow, never inflow

**Symptom:** After the dispatch-range leak below was fixed, a second, much smaller-magnitude leak remained: large, gravity-independent fill LOSSES (hundreds to low-thousands per tick) during active water pouring, always clustered around whatever cell happened to be the current flow-convergence point — never a single fixed cell, and never present on settled/resting water. A voxel-watch diagnostic (`FluidSimulation.watch_enabled`/`watch_chunk`/`watch_local`, plus an F9 crosshair-targeting keybind in `world.gd`) confirmed the watched cell itself stayed completely unremarkable throughout — the loss was landing on a shifting set of *different* voxels each tick, ruling out a single misbehaving cell. Confirmed independent of `gravity_bias`/`gravity_force` (magnitude unchanged with both at their minimum).

**Root cause:** `fluid_flux.comp`'s Scale pass only ever bounded what a voxel could *give away*: `s = min(1, fill(p) / total_outflow(p))`. Nothing bounded what a voxel could *receive*. At a flow-convergence point (e.g. under an active pour, or the bottom of a falling column), several different donor voxels can each independently stay within their own Scale-approved outflow budget while their *combined* delivery to one shared receiver still exceeds the 255 fill cap. Transport's `clamp(old_fill + delta, 0, 255)` then silently discards the excess, with no debit anywhere in the system to balance it — a real, uncorrected loss. This explains every observed property: losses (not gains, since convergence funnels many donors into few receivers) that shift tick to tick (whichever cell is the current convergence point), scale with how much simultaneous flow is happening (more active donors → more chances to overcommit a receiver), are silent on settled water (no convergence when nothing's flowing), and don't respond to gravity tuning (a geometric convergence problem, not a force-magnitude problem).

**Fix:** Added a second, symmetric cap — Scale-Inflow — mirroring the existing Scale-Outflow logic from the receiver's side: `s_in = min(1, (255 - fill(p)) / total_inflow(p))`, applied to every face representing inflow to `p`. This **must** run as its own GPU dispatch, fully synced after Scale-Outflow completes, not merged into the same pass — a single flux lane can simultaneously be "outflow" from its owner's perspective and "inflow" to its `+axis` neighbour's perspective, and two different invocations writing the same lane in the same pass would race. Running it sequentially after Scale-Outflow avoids that: Scale-Inflow reads the already-outflow-scaled values, and shrinking a lane further there can never re-violate Scale-Outflow's already-satisfied guarantee (shrinking only ever reduces a donor's total requested outflow). Within the Scale-Inflow pass itself, the same sign-based mutual-exclusion proof Scale-Outflow already relies on holds with inflow/outflow roles swapped, so it's race-free on its own too.

Pass numbering changed: `0=Pass A, 1=Scale-Outflow, 2=Scale-Inflow (new), 3=Transport` (was `0=Pass A, 1=Scale, 2=Transport`). `FluidSimulation.step()` now dispatches four passes per sub-step instead of three, each with its own submit/sync.

**Rule:** A conservation guard that only bounds one side of a transfer (donor-only, in this case) isn't sufficient — check whether the *other* side needs symmetric bounding too, especially at fan-in/fan-out points (multiple donors → one receiver, or the reverse). When two per-voxel caps must both apply to the same shared resource (a flux lane, here), and both directions could legitimately need to shrink it, don't try to enforce both in one pass — sequence them as separate, fully-synced dispatches so each pass keeps its own single-writer-per-lane guarantee.

### BUG (2026-07-02): fluid flux leak — Pass A computed flux against un-dispatched neighbour chunks

**Symptom:** Unbounded, one-sided fill GAIN reported by the automatic conservation checker, never balanced by a corresponding loss. Small and intermittent at first ("takes a minute to get the reaction started"), then explosive — totals climbing into the thousands per tick, eventually overflowing the changelog. A location-tracking diagnostic (`GainTracker`, binding 10 in `fluid_flux.comp`) showed every large gain clustered at local `y=30`/`y=31` — the topmost layer of a chunk, i.e. a Y-axis chunk boundary — across many different chunk positions. Setting `gravity_force` to 0 did **not** stop it (ruled out gravity as the cause; it only changed the cosmetic shape of the pour).

**Failed attempt (kept, but insufficient alone):** Zeroing a paged-but-excluded chunk's `flux_buf` data at the start of each `step()` (theory: stale flux surviving the idle-guard grace period was being read and credited by an active neighbour without ever being debited). This didn't fix the leak — confirmed by user testing with unchanged leak character afterward. It's still logically sound as a defense-in-depth measure and was kept, but it addressed the wrong layer: it cleaned up flux *data*, not the *page-table entry* that Pass A's neighbour check now depends on (see fix below).

**Root cause:** `fluid_flux.comp` Pass A gated its whole per-axis loop only on `my_page < 0` (the current voxel's *own* chunk). It never checked whether the *neighbour's* chunk was actually part of this tick's dispatch range (`flux_list`). `read_a(nb)` always succeeds regardless of paging — it's a plain world-position lookup into `voxels_a`. So a dispatched boundary voxel would happily compute a real, nonzero flux value using a neighbour's true fill data, and materialize/drain fill from itself accordingly — but if the neighbour's chunk wasn't in `flux_list` this tick (e.g. it's a stale page still in its idle-guard grace period, or simply 2 rings away from any active chunk), **no invocation ever runs for the neighbour's voxels this tick**, so the neighbour's side of the transfer (the debit or credit that should balance it) never happens. Net effect: mass created or destroyed from nothing, one-sided, every tick this condition holds.

This is self-reinforcing: each phantom materialization creates a newly-active voxel, which becomes part of `_active_chunks` next tick, which expands `flux_list`'s 1-ring neighbourhood one ring further out — so the same leak mechanism re-triggers at the new frontier, tick after tick, which is exactly why it "takes a minute to get started" (needs the active region to organically reach a boundary where a currently-undispatched neighbour holds real liquid) and then runs away once it does.

**Fix (two parts, must ship together):**
1. `shaders/fluid_flux.comp` Pass A: before computing accel for a face, look up the *neighbour's* chunk in `flux_page_table` (same pattern as `flux_lane()`) and treat `nb_page < 0` exactly like the existing solid-boundary case — zero the lane, skip. This requires `flux_page_table` to mean "in this tick's dispatch," not just "has ever had a page allocated."
2. `simulations/fluid_simulation.gd` `step()`: `flux_page_table` is now rebuilt every tick to match that meaning — every chunk in `flux_list` has its real page index re-written (`_write_flux_page_table_entry`) in case a previous tick unmapped it, and every paged chunk *not* in `flux_list` this tick gets its page table entry set to `-1` (in addition to the existing flux-data zeroing). The CPU-side `_flux_chunk_table` bookkeeping and the idle-guard eviction timing are untouched — this is a per-tick logical unmap/remap of the GPU-visible page table, not a change to actual page allocation lifetime.

**Rule:** Any shader that reads a neighbour's world-position data across a sparse/paged dispatch range must verify the neighbour is *also* within this tick's dispatch, not just that its own invocation is. Reading real data via a plain world-position lookup (`read_a`-style) will always "succeed" even for data that no corresponding GPU invocation will ever process this tick — that mismatch is invisible until you specifically check dispatch membership, not data validity.

### BUG: `Vector3i(0,0,0)` is falsy in GDScript — silently drops chunk (0,0,0)

**Symptom:** Sand in the exact center chunk (0,0,0) never simulated. Every other chunk worked. Placing sand there returned `[Edit] committed chunks: []`. The sand was visible (voxels_a was correct) but simulation never triggered and collision never rebuilt.

**Root cause:** `Dictionary.get(key)` returns the value if found, or `null` if missing. When the value IS `Vector3i(0,0,0)`, GDScript evaluates it as falsy — `not Vector3i.ZERO` is `true`. So `if not chunk_pos: continue` silently discarded the (0,0,0) result as if the key weren't in the dictionary. Two locations had this bug:
- `WorldOrchestrator._extract_changed_chunks` line: `if not chunk_pos: continue`
- `GranularSimulation._decode_dirty_bitmask` line: `if cp: out.append(cp)`

Both appear after `dict.get(slot)` where the slot maps to chunk (0,0,0).

**Fix:** Use `dict.get(key, null)` with an explicit sentinel default and compare `== null`:
```gdscript
var chunk_pos: Variant = slot_to_chunk.get(slot, null)
if chunk_pos == null:
    continue
```

**Rule:** Never use truthiness checks on values from a `Dictionary.get()` call when the value type can be zero or a zero vector. Always use `== null` or check `dict.has(key)` first.

### GOTCHA: GDScript integer division on negative values

`flat_idx / vpc` in GDScript uses truncating division (rounds toward zero), not floor division. For positive `flat_idx` values this is fine. If `flat_idx` ever becomes negative (e.g. from a corrupt changelog entry or a shader bug), the slot computation would be wrong. The GPU changelog should never produce negative `flat_idx` values — `world_to_flat` returns -1 for out-of-bounds which causes an early `return` in the shader before the changelog write — but worth knowing if debugging future slot mismatch issues.

### GOTCHA: GLSL reserved keywords as variable names

`flat` is a reserved GLSL interpolation qualifier and cannot be used as a variable name in compute shaders. Using it silently causes driver-dependent tokenization errors that are hard to trace. Always use ASCII-only characters in shader source — non-ASCII characters (em dashes, curly quotes) in comments can corrupt tokenization on some drivers.

### GOTCHA: `_slot_to_chunk` vs. `_chunk_to_slot` — two separate maps

Both maps must be kept in sync at all times. `_add_chunk_to_grid` writes both. `_remove_chunk_from_grid` erases both. `_init_grid` clears both. ### GOTCHA: Checkerboard idle guard requires 4 consecutive idle frames

The 2-frame guard (idle >= 2) was too short. The checkerboard skips one parity per frame, and two stacked grains at the same (x, z) share the same parity — one idle frame just means "wrong parity this frame." Without a sufficient guard, same-parity stacks froze after one uneventful dispatch. The guard was raised to 4 frames (covers both parities twice) to give boundary grains enough opportunities to move.

### BUG: Fluid trace cells (fill=1–31) created by flux and try_compress, fall off ledges

**Symptom:** Water placed at ledge edges produces nearly-invisible (dark) fill=1–15 voxels that fall freely off cliffs for a long time. Water placed via sphere tool spawns at fill<10. Holes fill extremely slowly.

**Root cause (flux):** The flux formula `gradient / 16` with MIN_FLOW_FILL=16 allowed a cell at fill=16 to send `16/16=1` unit to each of 4 air neighbors. Those air neighbors received fill=1 — nearly invisible, but still a real voxel subject to gravity. Additionally, the donor (fill=16) sent 1×4=4 units total, going to fill=12. The donor then couldn't spread further (below MIN_FLOW_FILL) but still fell vertically.

**Root cause (try_compress):** `try_compress` in fluid_sim.comp could leave the source with `new_my_fill = 1–31` after giving fill to the destination below. These trace-fill cells passed the `material_sim == LIQUID` check and fell via `try_move`.

**Root cause (total outflow):** With divisor=16 and 4 neighbors, a full cell (fill=255) sent `63×4=252` total, leaving itself at fill=3 — a trace cell at the original position.

**Fix (fluid_flux.comp pass 0):**
1. Changed divisor from 16 to 4 (4× faster equalization).
2. Rewrote pass 0 to compute all 4 neighbor fluxes, then cap total outflow at `my_fill - MIN_FILL (32)` to ensure the donor always retains at least fill=32.
3. For air targets only: skip the direction if computed flux < MIN_FILL (32) — prevents creating near-empty air cells.
4. Raised MIN_FILL from 16 to 32 (don't emit flux at all if below 32).

**Fix (fluid_sim.comp):**
1. Added `if (my_fill_v < 32u) return;` guard at top of main() — trace cells can't move at all.
2. In `try_compress`: if `new_my_fill > 0 && new_my_fill < 32`, snap to 0 (destroy the remainder). Tiny volume loss acceptable; prevents fill=1 ghost cells.

**Follow-up:** Pass 0 capping prevents the DONOR from going below MIN_FILL=32, but an existing liquid cell can still end up at fill=27 if it receives a small negative delta from pass 0 (gave away fill to 4 neighbors and the scaling rounded down). Fixed in pass 1: after `new_fill = clamp(old_fill + delta, 0, 255)`, snap `new_fill` to 0 if `0 < new_fill < 32`.

**Rule:** Any fill-transfer operation (flux, compress) must ensure the result is either ≥ MIN_FILL or exactly 0. Check at BOTH the source (pass 0 cap) and the result (pass 1 snap). The "middle ground" trace fills are inert, invisible, and hang permanently.

### BUG: Single water voxel explodes into a runaway fill=1 blob, changelog overflow

**Symptom:** Placing one water voxel produced a rapidly expanding blob that engulfed most of the visible terrain within seconds, converging to fill≈1 everywhere. `apply_changelog()` logged `changelog overflow (284752 entries); clamping to 131072` from what started as a single-voxel edit.

**Root cause — two compounding issues, both in Phase 7e's Transport pipeline (fluid_flux.comp):**

1. **`g_dt` default (6.0) was wildly oversized relative to the flux value scale.** Head ranges 0–255 for a single cell; `FILL_CAP` clamps a tick's flux to ±255. A freshly placed full droplet (`fill=255`) facing air computes `head(own)=255`, `head(air)=0` → `dh=255` → `f' = 255*6 = 1530`, instantly clamped to the maximum possible transfer (255) on the very first tick. Instead of building pressure gradually across many ticks (the entire point of persistent flux — see Phase 7d's root-cause writeup above), the donor could empty essentially its entire fill in a single tick.

2. **No floor on air-materialization.** Scale (Step 5) guarantees a donor voxel can never be asked to give more than it holds — but it does *not* guarantee the transferred amount is large enough to be worth spawning a new voxel for. A cell at `fill=1` can still legally give away that last unit (`s = min(1, 1/total_out)` permits it). With no floor, "give my last 1 unit, become 0, neighbour becomes 1" chains outward indefinitely — every newly materialized cell becomes an active candidate next tick and repeats the pattern, one air cell at a time, never settling. This is what produced the fill≈1 blob and the changelog explosion (hundreds of newly-materialized cells per tick, each triggering `chunks_committed` discovery, each activating more neighbouring chunks the tick after).

**Fix:**
- Lowered `FluidSimulation.g_dt` default from 6.0 to 0.15, tightened the export range from `(0, 50)` to `(0, 2)` — the useful range where flux builds up gradually instead of saturating instantly is well under 1.0.
- Added `MIN_MATERIALIZE = 16` in Transport's `is_air` branch: refuse to create a new liquid voxel if the transferred amount would leave it below this floor. The donor's outflow was already committed by Scale, so the discarded fill is a small, bounded, one-tick loss — not an unbounded leak, and not a case Scale's conservation guarantee was ever supposed to cover (it guarantees "can't overdraw," not "won't spread into monomolecular films").

**Rule — distinguish this from Phase 7d's MIN_FILL hacks.** Phase 7d's thresholds were emergency patches compensating for a system with *no* conservation guarantee. Phase 7e's `MIN_MATERIALIZE` is a deliberate design floor layered *on top of* a system that already conserves correctly — the same way real fluids don't spread into films a single molecule thick. Don't conflate "this system needs a threshold to not break" (7d) with "this system chooses a threshold for sane behavior" (7e) when reading old vs. new code.

```
tick 6: fill=51  (4 voxels)   — lost 51
tick 7: fill=1   (1 voxel)    — lost 50
```

Two independent test runs (mid-chunk placement, chunk-border placement) produced the **identical** total sequence — proof the loss was deterministic and unrelated to chunk boundaries, which ruled out a Scale/Transport cross-chunk bug before any shader code was touched.

**Root cause — NOT either of the two theories originally guessed.** The legacy vertical-CAS shader (`fluid_sim.comp`) was still dispatched every sub-step, *before* Pass A/Scale/Transport ran, left over from Phase 7e's incremental rollout (Steps 4–6 explicitly kept it running alongside the new system — see each step's "interim state" notes above). Its `try_compress` still contained the destructive snap-to-zero added earlier this session, before Phase 7e existed, to solve a completely different problem (trace cells falling off ledges under the old fill-diffusion model): `if (new_my_fill > 0u && new_my_fill < 32u) { new_my_fill = 0u; }`. As Transport correctly created more small-fill voxels each tick (exactly as flux distributes a droplet outward), vertical CAS's compress pass encountered them on the *next* tick and silently discarded up to 31 units per compression. Loss scaled with the growing voxel count in the logs above — corroborating evidence, not just correlation, once the mechanism was identified.

**Both original theories were wrong** (worth recording so they aren't re-tried): `head()`'s upward-only asymmetry is intentional and correct (a cell's own head query and a query *of* that cell from below are two different calls with different starting points, not a broken symmetry); `MIN_MATERIALIZE` discards were real but far too small (max 15/tick, discarded only on failed materialization) to account for losses of 35–63 per tick.

**Fix:** `fluid_sim.comp` deleted entirely (this is also Phase 7e Step 7, arrived at earlier than planned because the data made the case decisively). All vertical-CAS scaffolding stripped from `fluid_simulation.gd` (`_pipeline`, `_zero_pipeline`, `_chunk_list_buf`, `_zero_chunk_list_buf`, `_global_params_buf`, and their associated build/upload functions). Y (vertical) flow is now handled entirely by `fluid_flux.comp`'s Y-axis flux — the same head-driven mechanism as X/Z, already proven conservation-safe by Scale.

**Rule:** when two systems can move the same conserved quantity, and only one of them is being actively verified, the other is a live suspect by default — "interim state, still dispatched for now" notes need to be treated as a todo with real urgency, not background noise, once the new system is far enough along to be measured.

### BUG (RESOLVED, 2026-07-02): Water fully vanishes to zero — stale flux survives voxel death

**Symptom:** After deleting `fluid_sim.comp` (previous entry), a single droplet no longer stopped at fill=1 — it went all the way to complete disappearance. Step-by-step data: `255 → 255(4vox) → 201(3vox) → 201(9vox) → 138(6vox) → 48(7vox) → 15(1vox) → 0 → 0`. A large sphere test (14,147 initial liquid voxels, `fill=3,607,485` total) showed the same shape at scale — a slow, steady, unrecoverable drain from 3.6M down to ~19K over roughly 90 stepped ticks, never stabilizing.

**Root cause.** Pass A's early-return for non-liquid cells — `if (mat_id == 0u || material_sim.data[mat_id] != SIM_LIQUID) return;` — meant that the instant a liquid voxel drained to `fill=0` and became air, **nothing ever touched its 3 owned flux lanes again**. But Scale and Transport keep reading a cell's owned-face storage regardless of whether the owner is still alive — a dead cell's last flux value is frozen permanently, and any live neighbor computing its own Transport delta keeps applying that stale, never-refreshed value *every single tick*, forever. This is the second half of Step 6b's flux-invalidation requirement: the existing Pass A code already handled "my neighbour became incompatible" (zeroes the face), but never handled "I myself just died" — the far more common case, since it fires every time flux successfully drains a cell to zero, which is the normal, correct, desired outcome of the whole system working.

**Why totals reached exactly zero and not some equilibrium:** once enough cells die and leave stale claims behind, live neighbors keep bleeding fill into frozen phantom obligations that are never revisited or corrected, compounding tick over tick with no counterbalancing mechanism — a one-way ratchet toward zero rather than a bounded loss.

**Fix:** Pass A now zeroes a cell's 3 owned lanes whenever it finds that cell is *not currently liquid* (covers death, displacement, and edits uniformly — anything that isn't `SIM_LIQUID` gets the same treatment), instead of silently skipping it:
```glsl
if (mat_id == 0u || material_sim.data[mat_id] != SIM_LIQUID) {
    for (int axis = 0; axis < 3; axis++) {
        flux_buf.data[my_page * vpc * 3 + my_local * 3 + axis] = 0;
    }
    return;
}
```
Since Pass A runs over the full active+neighbor dispatch range every tick regardless of current material, this invalidation now happens automatically the very next tick after any cell dies — no separate signal or edit-hook needed.

**Rule:** "early return for the cells I don't care about" is a dangerous default in a system with *persistent* per-cell state — a pass that skips a cell entirely also skips cleaning up after it. Any persistent buffer (this session: `_flux_buf`) needs an explicit answer to "what happens to this cell's stored state when it stops being a cell this system cares about," not just "what happens while it's alive."

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): `MIN_MATERIALIZE` was a one-sided conservation leak — replaced with a read-side deadzone

**Symptom:** Re-ran the single-droplet test after the stale-flux fix above. Result: **bit-for-bit identical** tick sequence to the pre-fix run — `255 → 255(4vox) → 201(3vox) → 201(9vox) → 138(6vox) → 48(7vox) → 15(1vox) → 0(0vox) → 0`. A large sphere test settled into a stable, uniform `fill≈1.7` plateau with `flux=0` everywhere (a genuine rest state, not still actively leaking — just reached after heavy loss along the way).

**Confirmed via the debug counter** (`DebugCounters`, binding 9 in `fluid_flux.comp`; `FluidSimulation.debug_dump_flux_stats()`, bound to `F6`), which measures exactly how much fill `MIN_MATERIALIZE` discards each tick. Re-running the droplet test with it active:

| Tick transition | Fill loss | Discard counter |
|---|---|---|
| 255→255 | 0 | 0 |
| 255→201 | 54 | 54 |
| 201→201 | 0 | 0 |
| 201→138 | 63 | 63 |
| 138→48 | 90 | 90 |
| 48→15 | 33 | 36 (float-rounding noise, negligible) |
| 15→0 | 15 | 15 |

Near-exact match every tick. The sphere test discarded 2,893,122 in a single accumulated read against a total loss of roughly 3.58M over the run — same story at scale. `MIN_MATERIALIZE` accounts for essentially all of the measured loss; the earlier stale-flux bug was real but not the dominant factor here.

**Why it's a leak, not just a bound that turned out bigger than expected — structural, not just numerical.** The floor (`if (is_air && new_fill < MIN_MATERIALIZE) return;`) only ever gates the *receiving* air cell. The *donor* voxel's own delta is computed and applied completely independently, the moment it's liquid — nothing gates a liquid cell losing fill to match. Concretely: `delta -= flux_buf.data[...]` for the donor's owned faces happens unconditionally in every liquid voxel's own Transport execution; whether the *neighbor* on the receiving end successfully materializes is irrelevant to the donor's own delta. So the donor always pays, and the receiver sometimes doesn't collect — asymmetric by construction. The original comment justifying this floor called it "a small, bounded, one-tick loss, not an unbounded leak" — the "bounded" half was never actually true, because nothing bounds the *donor's* side to match the receiver's refusal.

**My read, offered as a starting point, not a decision:** this floor should either (a) be removed outright, trusting Scale's conservation guarantee the way the rest of Phase 7e already does — the concern that motivated it (Phase 7d's "give my last 1 unit forever" chains) doesn't obviously reapply here since Scale + head-driven Pass A is a fundamentally different, physically-grounded mechanism, not the old biased CAS system; or (b) stay, but become symmetric — the donor's own outflow computation would need to respect the same floor Transport's receiver-side check applies, likely meaning the threshold has to move earlier, into Scale or Pass A, where both sides of a face are still being decided together rather than resolved independently per-cell in Transport. I don't have strong confidence between these without more testing (specifically: does removing the floor reintroduce visually-annoying trace droplets now that the underlying mechanism is physically damped, rather than a biased runaway?).

**Review verdict (Fable 5, 2026-07-02):** the floor is a real leak and must go. On the fix itself: replace `MIN_MATERIALIZE` (per-cell, receiver-side, lossy) with a per-face **read-side deadzone** applied wherever a flux lane is read, in both Scale and Transport — `eff_flux(f) = (abs(f) >= MIN_FLOW) ? f : 0`. Both sides of a face read the same stored value through the same pure function, so donor and receiver always agree — symmetric by construction, zero volume loss, no writes, no race, no extra pass. It also preserves the persistence property: sub-threshold flux keeps accumulating in the lane across ticks (equilibrium ≈ `dh·g_dt/(1−damp)`), so small pressure differences can still eventually seep, whereas a write-side snap would permanently block them. A per-face floor of `MIN_FLOW` also guarantees any single-donor materialized cell starts ≥ `MIN_FLOW`, so the old receiver-side check is deleted outright, not moved.

**Applied (2026-07-02).** `fluid_flux.comp`: added `const int MIN_FLOW = 16;` and `int eff_flux(int raw)` right after `head()`. Scale's `total_out` computation (both the owned-face loop and the neighbour-face loop) now reads through `eff_flux()`; its scaling-write loops only touch a lane when `eff_flux(raw)` is nonzero (a sub-threshold lane is never written at all, so it keeps accumulating under Pass A's normal integration rather than being nudged by a budget cap it was never part of). Transport's `delta` computation reads every term through `eff_flux()` too. The old `MIN_MATERIALIZE` block and its `debug_counters` atomic (which had done its job — it's what produced the table above) were both removed, along with the now-fully-unreferenced `DebugCounters` buffer (binding 9) in both `fluid_flux.comp` and `fluid_simulation.gd` — leaving it declared-but-unused would have reproduced the exact "SPIR-V strips it, GDScript still supplies a uniform for it, `uniform_set_create` fails" crash fixed earlier this session.

**Known narrow edge case, accepted as-is:** if an air cell receives from multiple donors simultaneously with opposing signs — e.g. `+17` from one neighbour and `−16` from another, each individually above `MIN_FLOW` — the *net* `delta` could be a small value like `+1`, producing a small materialized cell despite every individual contribution clearing the threshold. This requires simultaneous opposing flows from different neighbours and is self-limiting rather than runaway (nothing like the old unbounded "give my last unit forever" chain); not worth a second gate for.

Fable 5's verdict also referenced two upstream root-cause bug entries ("Pass A's non-liquid early-out," "Y-axis flux has no gravity offset") that didn't exist in this document at the time — see the entry immediately below, which writes both of them up. They turned out to be the same root cause, not two separate ones.

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): Pass A's liquid-only gating made flow one-directional — water could only expand +X/+Y/+Z, never fall

**Symptom:** reported directly by the user: placing a sphere of water produced a hard cutoff — it only ever expanded up, right, and forward (+Y, +X, +Z), never backward, left, or down (−Z, −X, −Y). Critically, the sphere never fell to the ground; it stayed floating exactly at its placement height indefinitely, expanding sideways in only three of six possible directions. This is the same root cause Fable 5's review verdict (previous entry) referenced as "Pass A's non-liquid early-out makes flow into air impossible in the −X/−Y/−Z directions (water literally cannot fall)" and "the Y-axis flux has no gravity offset" — investigation confirmed both symptoms trace to one bug, not two.

**Root cause.** Pass A computed acceleration for a voxel's 3 *owned* faces (+X, +Y, +Z), and only ran that computation when the *current* thread's own voxel was liquid — a non-liquid cell hit an early return that zeroed its lanes and skipped the loop entirely. But by the face-ownership convention (documented since Step 2), the face between a liquid cell `p` and its `-X` neighbour `q` is **owned by `q`**, not `p` — flow from `p` backward into `q` can only ever be expressed as a negative value on `q`'s own `+X` face, computed by `q`'s own Pass A execution. If `q` is air, that execution short-circuits before ever reaching the accel computation. Net effect: a liquid voxel can push into a `+axis` air neighbour (that computation happens from the liquid side, already worked), but nothing ever computes the symmetric case of an air neighbour on the `-axis` side accepting inflow — because that's a *different* thread's voxel, and that thread bailed out immediately for being air. This affected all three axes identically, but Y is where it's most visible: gravity is strictly a `-Y` phenomenon, so "flow only works in the positive direction" is indistinguishable from "there is no gravity" from the player's perspective.

**Fix.** Changed Pass A's gating from "am I liquid" to a symmetric per-axis check on both sides of the face: solid on either side → zero, always (no flow ever possible there); both sides air → zero (nothing to move); both sides liquid but different materials → zero (kept separate, unchanged from before); otherwise — liquid facing air, air facing liquid, or same liquid on both sides — compute `dh = head(gpos, active_mat) - head(nb, active_mat)` and integrate, exactly as before, just no longer gated on which side of the face the current thread happens to be looking from. `active_mat` resolves to whichever side actually holds the liquid, so `head()` reads the correct column regardless of orientation.

**Gravity falls out for free — no special case needed.** Because `head()` is used uniformly for all three axes (X, Y, Z) with no per-axis branching, downward flow isn't a separate mechanism or a constant force: a liquid cell always has `head > 0`, empty air below it always reads `head == 0` (see `head()`'s definition — non-matching material returns 0 immediately), so `dh` between a liquid cell and the air below it is always negative, driving flow downward, proportional to the liquid's actual head/depth rather than a fixed magnitude. This is why the "Y-axis flux has no gravity offset" framing and the "Pass A non-liquid early-out" framing turned out to be the same bug: once air cells can compute their own owned faces at all, gravity requires zero additional code — the existing head-difference formula already produces it correctly on the Y axis exactly like it does on X and Z.

**Self-invalidating by construction — the old Step 6b "I died" branch became unnecessary.** The previous code had an explicit early branch: if a cell isn't currently liquid, zero its 3 owned lanes and return, specifically to stop a dead cell's stale flux from being read forever (documented in an earlier bug entry, "stale flux survives voxel death"). That branch is gone now — and doesn't need replacing. Since every voxel (liquid or air) now recomputes its owned faces fresh every single tick based on current neighbour state, a cell that dies (or gets edited, or displaced) simply gets its faces recomputed correctly the very next tick, whatever its new state is. Invalidation was a workaround for a system that skipped updating dead cells; a system where every relevant cell is updated every tick doesn't need a separate invalidation mechanism.

**Follow-up (2026-07-02, same day): the "known residual limitation" above was worse than scoped and got its own fix.** Reported directly: water expanded correctly in all directions within a chunk, but hit a hard wall at chunk boundaries. Root cause: page allocation only ever covered `_active_chunks`. Positive-direction flow across a boundary stores on the *donor's* owned face — the donor's chunk is already active/paged, so it always worked and was never the problem. Negative-direction flow (including straight down) stores on the *receiver's* owned face, per face ownership — if that neighbouring chunk has never held water, it has no page, so Pass A's `if (my_page < 0) return;` fires for every voxel in it before the per-axis loop can even run. Critically, **this does not self-heal the way the original "1-tick lag" framing assumed**: self-healing requires a changelog entry to trigger discovery, and nothing is ever computed there in the first place, so no changelog entry is ever produced — it's a permanent wall for that direction, not a 1-tick delay.

**Fix:** `fluid_simulation.gd`'s `step()` now allocates pages for the full flux dispatch range (`_build_flux_chunk_list` output — active + all 6 face neighbours), not just `_active_chunks`. This closes the hole for all three negative axes uniformly (it's the same underlying mechanism, not a per-axis special case).

**This created a new problem, fixed in the same change:** proactively allocating pages for every neighbour means chunks that are adjacent to water but never actually receive any (e.g. a rock wall bordering a pond) would get a page and never have it freed — the existing eviction loop only walks `_active_chunks`, and a pure-neighbour chunk never enters that dict. Added a second, parallel idle-guard (`_flux_page_idle`, same `idle < 4` shape as the existing one) that tracks every paged chunk *not* currently active and frees its page after a few idle ticks — with enough grace period that a chunk materializing its first voxel this tick isn't yanked out from under itself before next tick's discovery recognizes it as active.

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): sphere expands as an octahedron instead of falling coherently — pressure difference alone isn't gravity

**Symptom:** with chunk-boundary flow working, a solid `fill=255` water sphere placed in mid-air didn't fall as a coherent mass — it immediately bulged outward in all six cardinal directions simultaneously, forming an octahedron ("a neat triangular bipyramid"). It never fell faster than it spread sideways.

**A separate coding agent's proposed fix** (`accel = -float(FILL_CAP) * g_dt` at a cited `fluid_flux.comp:201`) does not exist in the current file — traced to stale/different code predating today's Pass A rewrite. Its underlying instinct (Y needs *something* beyond pure pressure-difference) turned out to be partly right, but the specific mechanism (a `FILL_CAP`-scaled constant, unconditional regardless of neighbour fill) was correctly rejected — it would drain a `fill=10` cell exactly as hard as a `fill=255` one.

**Actual root cause:** `head(gpos, active_mat)` is a single scalar per cell — pressure due to the vertical column above it — and Pass A reused that *same* value identically for computing `dh` on all three axes (X, Y, Z). No directional bias existed anywhere in the accel formula. For a sphere, `head()` is largest at the bottom pole (the full vertical diameter of full cells sits above it) — but near a pole the horizontal cross-section is narrow, so that same maximum-pressure cell's horizontal neighbours are *also* air. The single large `head()` value pushed with equal, maximal strength into every exposed neighbour at once (down + several sideways), not preferentially downward — producing the octahedral bulge.

The deeper gap: real fluids have two distinct drivers — gravity (a constant body force, vertical only, independent of pressure) and the pressure gradient (arises from head differences, drives flow in all directions to equalize). These balance in hydrostatic equilibrium for a *supported* fluid, but for fluid in free-fall (nothing supporting it), gravity should dominate over lateral pressure-equalization. The system only ever had the pressure-gradient mechanism, with an identical constant (`g_dt`) on every axis — nothing made vertical respond faster than horizontal for a comparable `dh`, and nothing pulled a perfectly uniform mass down at all if its internal `dh` was near zero everywhere (amplifying zero is still zero).

**Fix — two complementary Y-axis-only terms, both layered on top of the existing `dh * g_dt` term, neither replacing it:**
```glsl
float axis_bias = (axis == 1) ? params.gravity_bias : 1.0;
float accel = float(dh) * params.g_dt * axis_bias;
if (axis == 1) {
    accel -= params.gravity_force;  // fy(p) positive = outflow upward, so downward push subtracts
}
```
- **`gravity_bias`** (multiplicative, default `3.0`, range `1.0–8.0`): once *any* pressure differential exists, vertical response wins the race against lateral spreading for the same `dh`.
- **`gravity_force`** (additive constant, default `10.0`, range `0.0–50.0`): a baseline downward nudge independent of `dh`, since `gravity_bias` alone only amplifies an *existing* differential — a perfectly uniform floating mass has ~zero internal `dh` everywhere and would never start falling from amplification alone. Both exported as `@export_range` on `FluidSimulation`, live-tunable via the Godot remote Inspector, matching the existing `damp`/`g_dt` pattern.

**Why this doesn't reintroduce the "drains disproportionately" flaw:** Scale (pass 1) still caps actual outflow at `my_fill` regardless of how large the raw computed accel is — a `fill=10` cell under a strong `gravity_force` still only ever loses up to 10 per tick in practice. The earlier rejected proposal's problem wasn't really about violating conservation (Scale already prevents that structurally); it was that a `FILL_CAP`-scaled constant, unconditionally applied, is a much cruder signal than a modest constant layered underneath the existing proportional term.

**`SimParams` grew from 44 to 52 bytes** (`fluid_flux.comp`: added `float gravity_bias;` and `float gravity_force;` after `g_dt`; `fluid_simulation.gd`: `_ready()`'s buffer allocation, `_upload_flux_params()`'s encoding, and the `_flux_params_buf` size comment all updated to match). Verified the 13-scalar/52-byte offsets line up exactly on both sides before testing — a struct/buffer size mismatch here would silently corrupt every param after `chunk_size` on the GPU side.

**No changes needed to Scale, Transport, or `head()`.** Scale/Transport are already axis-agnostic and read whatever ends up in `flux_buf` regardless of how it was computed. `head()`'s upward-only walk defines a correct, direction-independent physical quantity — the bug was in how that scalar was *applied* uniformly across axes in Pass A, not in how it was computed.

**Known secondary risk, not pre-emptively addressed:** does amplifying/adding-force-to Y suppress horizontal puddle-spreading once water lands and settles? Second-order: horizontal `dh*g_dt` is completely unchanged in absolute terms — a resting puddle has near-zero Y-axis `dh` against the floor already, so it spreads exactly as fast as before. In mixed scenarios (a mound still settling, both vertical and horizontal pressure present), more of the fluid's Scale-capped "response budget" goes vertical first — the intended fix, not a bug. If a real regression shows up, `debug_dump_flux_stats()` (`F6`) can compare per-axis lane magnitudes before/after to confirm, rather than pre-emptively adding a lateral counter-multiplier.

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): voxels with fill 1–14 permanently stuck in mid-air, never falling — `MIN_FLOW` deadzone traps low-fill cells

**Symptom:** reported while testing the gravity fix above, with a screenshot showing a tall thinning column of leftover water above a spread puddle — cursor on a voxel reading `Fill: 8`. User: voxels drop to fill 1–14 (even with more water directly adjacent/below) and then just stay stuck, never falling further, never handing off their remaining fill.

**Root cause — a real structural deadlock, not a slow drain.** `eff_flux(raw) = (abs(raw) >= MIN_FLOW) ? raw : 0` (`MIN_FLOW = 16`, added alongside the `MIN_MATERIALIZE` fix earlier today) is read symmetrically by both Scale and Transport. Scale caps a cell's total outflow at `my_fill` — so a cell with `fill=8` can never request more than 8 units of outflow, full stop, no matter how many ticks pass. Since `8 < MIN_FLOW`, that transfer is *always* invisible to `eff_flux()`, on every single tick, forever. The dispatch order makes this worse than a simple "small transfers never happen" limitation: Pass A integrates the raw stored value past 16 within 1–2 ticks (confirmed by hand-computing the geometric accumulation under `damp`/`gravity_force`), but **Scale runs before Transport in the same tick** and immediately re-scales that value back down to at most `my_fill` (< 16) before Transport ever gets a turn to read it. Next tick, Pass A grows it past 16 again from the shrunk base, Scale caps it back down again — an oscillating trap, not a threshold that eventually gets crossed. The comment justifying the original `MIN_FLOW=16` claimed sub-threshold flux "keeps accumulating... until it naturally crosses the threshold on its own" — that reasoning didn't account for Scale's same-tick, pre-Transport re-capping interacting with a cap that's itself below the threshold. Every fill value the user reported (1–14) was under 16 — not a coincidence, a direct prediction of this mechanism.

**Fix:** lower `MIN_FLOW` from 16 to 1 (`fluid_flux.comp`). This does **not** reintroduce the asymmetric `MIN_MATERIALIZE` leak fixed earlier — that leak came specifically from donor and receiver disagreeing about a value (one side saw it, the other didn't); `eff_flux()` applied identically on both sides is leak-free at any threshold, including a low one. The only thing a low threshold gives up is the original cosmetic goal (don't spawn dust-speck droplets in air) — and that concern is now substantially smaller given `gravity_bias`/`gravity_force` (previous entry) actively consolidate thin films instead of letting them spread indefinitely, which was the scenario `MIN_FLOW=16` was originally sized to prevent.

**No other changes needed.** Scale and Transport's logic is unchanged — only the threshold constant moved. Worth re-testing the earlier "trace droplet" concern now that both fixes are in (gravity fix + lowered deadzone) — if dust-speck droplets do turn out to be visually annoying in practice, the fix belongs in a *destination-aware* check (only gate genuinely new air→liquid materialization, never gate an already-existing liquid cell's continued movement) rather than raising `MIN_FLOW` back up, since raising it back up reproduces this exact bug.

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): raising `g_dt` freezes water into standing full-fill columns — `gravity_bias` amplification saturates away its own advantage

**Symptom:** while hand-tuning `gravity_bias`/`gravity_force`/`g_dt` live, raising `g_dt` produced tall, standing, full-`fill=255` columns/walls of water that simply stopped moving — screenshot showed pillar/arch-like frozen shapes with air gaps between them. Setting `g_dt` back to `0` let it "unstick" (briefly falling again), but placing more water and raising `g_dt` again reproduced the same frozen state.

**Root cause.** `gravity_bias` multiplies `g_dt` on the Y axis — at the *previous* export ranges (`g_dt` up to `2.0`, `gravity_bias` up to `8.0`), the effective Y-axis multiplier could reach `16`, meaning any `dh >= 16` (a very ordinary head difference, not an extreme one) instantly saturated flux to the `±FILL_CAP` clamp ceiling. Saturating isn't itself wrong — `FILL_CAP` clamping is a deliberate, correct per-tick limit. The actual bug: **once several competing directions all hit the exact same clamp ceiling simultaneously, they lose the relative distinction `gravity_bias` exists to create.** Scale divides a cell's outflow budget proportionally to each direction's *relative* magnitude — but if X, Y, and Z all read `255` because all three saturated, they're tied, and Scale splits the budget roughly evenly among them again, silently reintroducing the exact symmetric/octahedral-spreading problem `gravity_bias` was built to fix, just triggered by oversaturation instead of by the original missing-bias bug. Compounding this: `damp` carries a saturated value forward into the next tick, and a fresh, possibly differently-signed saturated `accel` gets added on top — the stored flux can oscillate between ticks without making net progress, which is exactly what "frozen in place" looks like from the outside. Resetting `g_dt` to `0` let the stored flux cleanly decay toward zero via `*damp` alone (no forcing to fight against), so raising it again started from a clean slate and worked — until enough ticks or new water pushed some region back into the saturating regime.

**Fix (initial attempt, insufficient):** narrowed the export ranges rather than changing the physics — `g_dt` from `[0, 2.0]` to `[0, 0.5]`, `gravity_bias` from `[1.0, 8.0]` to `[1.0, 5.0]`. Reasoning at the time: keep the combined `g_dt*gravity_bias` multiplier well under 1.0, mirroring the existing "keep g_dt well under 1.0" guideline.

**Follow-up (same day): still reproduced at `g_dt=0.2`, which is inside the narrowed range.** The range-narrowing missed the actual scale of the problem: `head()` adds 255 per full cell in a column, so even a **2-deep** column already produces `dh=510` on its bottom cell — at `g_dt=0.2, gravity_bias=3.0` (effective multiplier 0.6, saturation point `dh≈425`), that already saturates. Saturation isn't a rare edge case reachable only by cranking sliders too high — **it's the normal case for anything but a single shallow puddle voxel.** Narrowing the range made the corner harder to reach, not impossible, and any real body of water deeper than ~2 cells reaches it immediately regardless of how conservative the sliders are.

**Actual fix: give Y a taller clamp ceiling than X/Z, instead of sharing `FILL_CAP`.** The real bug was never "saturation happens" — `FILL_CAP` clamping every tick is correct and deliberate. The bug was that **X, Y, and Z all clamped to the exact same ceiling**, so the instant more than one axis saturated, they tied, and Scale's single proportional scale factor `s` (applied identically to every lane) had no way to tell them apart — collapsing straight back to symmetric/even distribution regardless of how large `gravity_bias` was set. Fix (`fluid_flux.comp`):
```glsl
float axis_cap = (axis == 1) ? float(FILL_CAP) * params.gravity_bias : float(FILL_CAP);
float new_f = old_f * params.damp + accel;
new_f = clamp(new_f, -axis_cap, axis_cap);
```
Y's raw stored value can now legitimately grow up to `FILL_CAP * gravity_bias` (up to 1275 at the new max `gravity_bias=5`) instead of being capped to the same 255 ceiling as X/Z. Scale doesn't need to know about this — it already operates on whatever raw magnitude is stored, with no assumption that any single lane is bounded to `FILL_CAP` specifically (verified: `FILL_CAP` doesn't appear anywhere in Scale's or Transport's logic). Because Scale's scale factor `s = my_fill / total_out` is applied uniformly, a larger raw Y value now receives a proportionally larger slice of the outflow budget even when every axis is fully saturated — preserving "down wins" all the way through saturation instead of losing it the moment multiple axes tie.

**Range narrowing from the initial attempt is kept, not reverted** — it's still a reasonable safety margin on top of the real fix, just not sufficient on its own.

### INVESTIGATION (OPEN, 2026-07-02): still freezes at `g_dt=0.2` after the per-axis clamp fix — changelog overflow found, cause not yet confirmed

**Symptom:** the per-axis clamp fix above (giving Y a taller ceiling) didn't resolve the freezing — same symptom reproduced at `g_dt=0.2`, well inside the narrowed range. User simultaneously noticed a console warning appearing every time it happens:
```
WorldOrchestrator: changelog overflow (259316 entries); clamping to 131072
```

**What this means, mechanically:** `apply_changelog()` silently *drops* any entries past `MAX_CHANGELOG_ENTRIES` — they're never applied to `voxels_a` at all, for any simulation sharing this buffer (granular, edits, fluid all write through the same one). If a large fraction of a falling sphere's legitimate fill updates get cut off this way every single tick, the visible result is indistinguishable from "frozen" even though the underlying flux math might be completely correct now.

**Two competing explanations, not yet distinguished:**
1. **The gravity fixes are working correctly** — more voxels are now legitimately active and moving every tick than in any scenario `MAX_CHANGELOG_ENTRIES=131072` (1 MB, sized for droplet-scale activity) was ever built for. The overflow is a capacity problem, not a physics problem — raising the cap should reveal correctly-falling water underneath.
2. **Still oscillating** — cells flip-flopping tick to tick without net convergence would *also* generate a large, sustained changelog count every tick (each flip is a fresh write), which would look identical to "legitimately lots of water moving" from the overflow warning alone. If this is what's happening, raising the cap just makes the oscillation more visible/expensive, not fixed.

**Applied so far (capacity side, safe regardless of which explanation is correct):** raised `MAX_CHANGELOG_ENTRIES` (`WorldOrchestrator.gd`) and every shader's matching `MAX_CHANGELOG` constant (`fluid_flux.comp`, `granular_sim.comp`, `edit_apply.comp` — all three write through the same shared buffer, so all three must match the GDScript-side allocation or a shader could write past what's actually allocated) from `131072` to `524288` (1 MB → 4 MB, trivial GPU memory cost, comfortably above the measured 259316-entry spike).

**Added a diagnostic to distinguish the two explanations, not yet interpreted:** `fluid_simulation.gd`'s Transport dispatch now prints the raw changelog entry count every sub-step (`[FluidTransport] changelog entries: N`), not just on overflow. Meant to be read via the existing `P` (pause) + `Right Arrow` (`step_once()`) workflow: a count that spikes once as a mass starts consolidating and then drops toward zero as it settles supports explanation 1 (capacity was the whole problem); a count that stays large and roughly constant tick after tick without decaying supports explanation 2 (still oscillating, cap increase just delays the symptom).

**Next step:** re-run the same `g_dt=0.2` repro with the raised cap and watch the printed counts. Not resolving this open item until that data comes back.

**Update — a third, distinct mechanism found, likely the dominant contributor:** while explanation 2 above ("still oscillating") was framed as a vague catch-all, the user's follow-up report ("water on the ground is always moving, never really settles... clogs the changelog with water that's just moving horizontally") pointed at something much more specific and provable — see the next entry. Both explanations 1 and 2 above may still be partially true, but this third mechanism is confirmed by direct calculation, not inference from a warning message, and is very likely what's actually filling the changelog on *settled* ground water specifically (as opposed to the still-actively-falling sphere, which is a separate, legitimate source of volume).

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): water on flat ground never settles — `round(old_f * damp)` has a fixed point, decay can get permanently stuck at 1–16

**Symptom:** reported directly: "water on the ground is always moving, it never really settles... it always clogs the changelog with water that's just moving horizontally." Got *worse*, not better, immediately after lowering `MIN_FLOW` from 16 to 1 (the low-fill-stuck-cell fix, two entries up) — a strong clue, since that fix specifically stopped filtering out the 1–16 magnitude range.

**Root cause — provable by direct calculation, not inference.** `flux_buf`'s persistence update was `flux_buf.data[lane] = int(round(old_f * params.damp + accel))`. For `damp = 0.97` and any integer magnitude `n` in `[1, 16]`, `round(n * 0.97) == n` — the decay term rounds right back up to its starting value. Concretely: `round(1 * 0.97) = round(0.97) = 1`; `round(16 * 0.97) = round(15.52) = 16`. **Decay alone can never reach exactly zero for any residual in this range** — it's a genuine fixed point of the update formula, not a slow asymptote. On a settled, flat puddle with no real pressure differential (`dh≈0`, `accel≈0`), any leftover residual flux in this range sits there forever, gets read by Scale/Transport every tick, and applied as a small (but real, nonzero) fill shift — perpetual, pointless shuffling that never converges, and a genuine changelog entry every tick for every affected voxel.

**Why lowering `MIN_FLOW` made it worse, not better:** `MIN_FLOW = 16` was, by coincidence, filtering out exactly this stuck range (`eff_flux()` treats anything with magnitude `< 16` as invisible) — so the rounding bug existed the whole time but had zero visible effect, because Scale and Transport never saw values in the range where it could get stuck. Lowering the threshold to 1 (needed to fix low-fill cells getting permanently trapped) simultaneously unmasked this completely different, previously-dormant bug across *every* settled body of water in the world at once — explaining both "why does ground water never settle" and "why did the changelog overflow get worse right after the last fix" in one mechanism.

**Fix:** truncate the decay term toward zero instead of rounding it, before adding the fresh `accel` contribution:
```glsl
int decayed = int(old_f * params.damp);   // truncation, not round — guarantees monotonic decay
float new_f = float(decayed) + accel;
new_f = clamp(new_f, -axis_cap, axis_cap);
flux_buf.data[lane] = int(round(new_f));
```
`int()` on a float truncates toward zero in GLSL (both directions — `int(-0.97) = 0`, not `-1`), so decay always shrinks in magnitude every tick with no fixed point: `int(1.0 * 0.97) = 0` (fully resolved in one step), `int(16.0 * 0.97) = 15` (still shrinking, not stuck). Only the carried-over persistence term needed this — `accel` (genuinely new forcing computed fresh this tick, not decay) still gets ordinary rounding on the final sum.

**Answers a design question that came up alongside this:** does a flux transfer have to go through the changelog? Yes — it's the only mechanism that updates `voxels_a` (the canonical GPU state the renderer reads), and skipping it for "merely cosmetic" transfers isn't actually possible without the render showing stale water. The fix belongs at the source (stop the spurious transfers from happening at all), not in routing around the changelog.

### BUG (FIXED, 2026-07-02): unbounded, one-sided fill creation at chunk-dispatch boundaries — stale flux pages read but never debited

**Symptom:** caught immediately by the new automatic conservation checker (previous tooling entry) — a sustained stream of `[Conservation] fill gained this tick: N` with **no corresponding losses ever printed**, growing without bound, from placing just a few droplets. Reported alongside a second, seemingly unrelated symptom: digging a deep hole and pouring water in, the water level rose barely at all, or extremely slowly.

**Root cause.** `flux_lane()` (the shader helper both Scale and Transport use to look up a neighbouring chunk's owned face) only checks whether that neighbour chunk **has a page allocated** — it does not check whether that chunk was **actually processed this tick**. The chunk-boundary fix from earlier today (giving every neighbour in the flux dispatch range a page, and separately, an idle-guard grace period before freeing pages that never became active) means a chunk can fall out of `flux_list` (active + 6 face neighbours, rebuilt fresh every tick) while still holding a page for a few more ticks. While it sits in that grace window, **Pass A never revisits it** (it isn't in the dispatch), so its last-computed flux values are frozen exactly where they were. But an *active* neighbouring chunk's Transport execution reads that frozen value via its own neighbour-owned-face lookup and credits itself — and since **Transport only ever reads `flux_buf`, it never writes to it**, nothing consumes or clears that value once read. The same active neighbour can read and credit itself from the identical stale number again the very next tick, and the tick after that, for the entire grace window — a real, repeatable, never-debited source of fill. With water constantly active near chunk boundaries, there's a continuous supply of newly-stale chunks feeding this, which is why the total kept climbing with no sign of stopping rather than settling at some fixed leaked amount.

**Why the hole-filling symptom is the same bug, not a separate one:** the water level in a deliberately dug hole is governed by real, correctly-computed downward flow (the gravity fixes earlier today) — comparatively modest and gradual, exactly as designed. The conservation total, however, was being inflated separately and much faster by phantom credits happening at essentially arbitrary chunk boundaries, unrelated to the pour location. Total fill skyrocketing while a deliberately-targeted hole barely rises is exactly what you'd see if most of the "new" fill was appearing somewhere other than where it was poured.

**Fix:** zero a paged chunk's flux data the instant it drops out of the current tick's dispatch range, rather than waiting for the idle-guard grace period to expire before freeing the page. This does not remove the grace period's own purpose (avoiding page alloc/free churn for chunks that briefly flicker in and out of the active set) — the page allocation itself still survives a few idle ticks for reuse efficiency. Only the *data* is invalidated the moment the chunk stops being actively recomputed, since stale-but-nonzero data is unsafe to leave readable by neighbours regardless of how briefly the chunk stays out of rotation. Implemented in `fluid_simulation.gd`'s `step()`, right after this tick's page allocation and before the Pass A/Scale/Transport sub-step loop: build the set of chunks in `flux_list`, then for every currently-paged chunk *not* in that set, zero its `vpc*3` flux lanes via `buffer_update`.

**Rule for the rest of this codebase's paging pattern:** "has a page" and "was processed this tick" are two different questions, and any shader logic that reads a neighbour's persistent per-chunk state through a page lookup needs to treat them as such — a page surviving for reuse efficiency does not mean the data behind it is still valid to read.

**Update (same day) — this fix did NOT resolve the leak.** Re-tested with the conservation checker running: same qualitative pattern (sustained one-sided gains, hundreds to low-thousands per tick, still climbing, never a single loss printed). The stale-page-during-grace-period mechanism above may still be real and worth keeping fixed, but it's evidently not the (only) dominant source. Rather than propose a fourth blind fix, added a location tracker instead of continuing to guess.

### TOOLING: gain-location tracker (added 2026-07-02) — pinpoints WHERE, not just how much

`fluid_flux.comp` binding 10 (`GainTracker`, 8 bytes: `flat_idx`, `amount`) — Transport does a plain (non-atomic, intentionally not synchronized against races — good enough for "which chunk to go look at," not meant to be a perfectly deterministic single winner) write whenever a voxel's applied gain exceeds the current tracked maximum for the tick. `FluidSimulation.step()` decodes `flat_idx` back into a chunk position + local `(x,y,z)` (same slot/local decomposition pattern as `_decode_dirty_bitmask`) and prints it alongside the conservation total, only when the conservation check has already found a nonzero delta:

```
[Conservation] fill gained this tick: 674
[Conservation]   biggest single gain: 210 at chunk (3, -1, 2) local (17, 4, 9)
```

### GOTCHA: `SimulationRuntime._active` iterator invalidation

GPU simulations emit `chunks_committed` inside `step()`, which triggers `_on_chunks_committed`, which may `append` to `_active`. Iterating `_active` directly causes iterator invalidation. Fix: snapshot `_active` before the loop with `.duplicate()` and do a post-loop merge for any sims added mid-frame.
